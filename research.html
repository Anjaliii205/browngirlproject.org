Research

Our Research

Brown Girl Project conducts qualitative and participatory research grounded in the lived experiences of women of color.

Our investigations begin with open-ended questions, allowing participants' experiences to inform the direction and findings of the research.

Investigation 01  |  2026

Do you identify as a woman of color — and if so, how?

22 Participants  ·  Qualitative Research  ·  United States

Our first investigation sought to understand what it means to identify as a woman of color in the United States, beginning with participants' own interpretations of that identity.

Rather than approaching women of color through a predetermined framework of marginalization, the investigation created space for participants to explore their everyday experiences, relationships, perceptions, and understandings of themselves.

The intention was not to establish a universal experience of being a woman of color, but to investigate the different ways in which that identity is understood and lived.

Methods

How we conducted the research.

The investigation engaged 22 participants through focus groups and individual interviews.

Using open-ended, conversation-based research methods, participants were invited to reflect on their experiences and explore how they understood their own identities.

Qualitative thematic analysis was used to identify recurring patterns, tensions, and differences across participant accounts.

Findings

What we found.

One of the central observations emerging from the investigation was that while participants could recognize themselves as women of color, some questioned whether their own experiences were sufficient to claim that identity.

The investigation identified four emerging themes.

Identity Gatekeeping

How participants understood who could identify as a woman of color and the perceived conditions surrounding that identity.

Privilege & Self-Exclusion

How perceptions of privilege and proximity to whiteness influenced participants' understanding of the legitimacy of their own experiences.

Internalized Pressure

The expectations participants experienced around representing their racial, cultural, or gender identities.

Neutral vs. Lived Patterns

The relationship between experiences participants described as ordinary and the broader patterns emerging through their accounts.

These themes reflect the experiences shared within this investigation and are not intended to represent all women of color.

Research Ethics

How we handle what you share.

Participation in BGP research is always voluntary and requires informed consent. Research is returned to the communities it studies — not just published for institutional audiences.

Participation is voluntary and can be withdrawn at any time
Identifying details are not made public without explicit permission
Notes and recordings are used solely for research and analysis
Findings are shared back with participants before broader publication
Research is written in accessible language for non-academic audiences
