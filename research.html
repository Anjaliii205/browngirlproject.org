Our Research

Investigation 01  |  2026

Do you identify as a woman of color — and if so, how?

Our first investigation explored what it means to be a woman of color in the United States, beginning with participants' own understanding of their identities and lived experiences.

Rather than approaching participants through a predetermined framework of marginalization, we sought to understand the full range of their experiences — the ordinary, the positive, the negative, and the complexities of everyday life.

22 Participants  ·  Qualitative Research  ·  United States
Read the Full Report
Methods

How we conducted the research.

Through focus groups and individual interviews, participants were invited to explore their experiences through open-ended conversations.

Qualitative thematic analysis was used to identify recurring patterns, tensions, and differences across participant accounts.

Findings

What we found.

While participants recognized themselves as women of color, some questioned whether their experiences were sufficient to claim that identity.

Four themes emerged from the investigation:

Identity Gatekeeping

Who feels able to identify as a woman of color?

Privilege & Self-Exclusion

How does perceived privilege influence the legitimacy of one's experiences?

Internalized Pressure

What expectations come with representing an identity or community?

Neutral vs. Lived Patterns

What do experiences described as ordinary reveal about everyday life?

Research Ethics

How we handle what you share.

Participation in BGP research is always voluntary and requires informed consent. Research is returned to the communities it studies — not just published for institutional audiences.

Participation is voluntary and can be withdrawn at any time
Identifying details are not made public without explicit permission
Notes and recordings are used solely for research and analysis
Findings are shared back with participants before broader publication
Research is written in accessible language for non-academic audiences
Beyond the Report

Following the investigation, we translated participant experiences into accessible public-facing materials, sharing their perspectives and extending the conversation beyond the written report.
